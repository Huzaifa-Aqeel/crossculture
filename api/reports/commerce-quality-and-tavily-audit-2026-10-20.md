# Native commerce quality and Tavily audit — 20 October 2026

## Method

Run the signed-in public API with ordinary US brand names, no supplied partner or product, and live Qloo, Shopify Catalog, and Tavily. Use both commerce objectives across different product categories. Inspect only selected exact offers, saved assessment rationales, current-offer states, cited public links, and generated concepts. Shopify search results, images, and full product payloads remain transient. Temporary users are deleted after each run.

Judge each observed selected pair against the v1.1 assessment gates: correct merchant/seller/variant, distinct product roles, concrete incremental benefit, objective fit, and claim scope. Record whether the app's outcome is defensible, too permissive, or too conservative. Judge concepts for product grounding and unsupported factual implications. These are manual engineering labels on a bounded sample, not population precision or recall estimates.

Tavily evidence is counted from the local usage ledger and search-cache metadata. A fresh provider request, cached query, inspected source, and source that actually appears in the opportunity are distinct events. First-party site/UCP checks are recorded separately from Tavily.

## Runs and findings

### Fishwife — food, Product Bundle

An ordinary name search selected Fishwife's native Qloo brand. The first signed-in attempt made one fresh Tavily identity search and stopped at confirmation. The inspected Tavily lead included `https://eatfishwife.com/`; the resolver rejected it because the domain was not `fishwife.com`. This was a false identity rejection, not a cultural or product verdict.

The resolver now treats inspected Tavily pages as bounded site hypotheses and requires the site's own business name plus a linked UCP shop before acceptance. A fresh signed-in attempt confirmed `https://eatfishwife.com` with the same native Qloo ID. Its first completed Product Bundle run made five fresh Tavily identity searches for recommended entities, but none passed merchant verification and no Catalog calls or product assessments occurred. That observed Qloo candidate pool included institutions and services; it does not support a claim that Fishwife has no viable partner.

The same run had rejected AllTrails because its official page title begins `AllTrails:` and the first-party parser only split a colon surrounded by spaces. A focused live check after correcting that parser verified `https://www.alltrails.com` with `https://shop.alltrails.com/.well-known/ucp`. A fresh Fishwife rerun then researched AllTrails with 17 Catalog calls. Four pairs were Needs Verification, including Fishwife Caviar × National Park Candle and Smoked Rainbow Trout (3-Pack) × AllTrails Sunscreen + Carabiner. On the selected-item titles and saved blank mechanism roles, these are defensible abstentions; neither shows a concrete Bundle benefit. The native Qloo relationship was present, but product fit was not established.

Shortlist Tavily vetting had returned `public_context_to_review` for AllTrails from older cached searches, but the retained links included a staff profile and a broad commentary article. Those pages did not themselves document a product collaboration or adverse event. The vetting filter now requires relevant inspected page text and excludes a personal profile. A read-only replay of the same cached Tavily responses returned `no_inspected_context`. It does not certify the candidate safe or unsuitable.
