# Independent native-commerce mechanism check — 21 October 2026

## Selection and scope

A seed roster and priority order were fixed from a SHA-256 draw before inspecting provider results: Bala, Ruggable, Rumpl, Swell, The Sill, Our Place, Blueland, Dr. Squatch. We tested the first three, then chose Our Place from the same preregistered roster for another product category; Swell and The Sill were not tested. The signed-in harness supplied only a brand name and objective; it did not inject a partner, product, variant, or Qloo result. All searches targeted the US. Bala had no exact native Qloo brand and was not substituted. Ruggable, Rumpl and Our Place resolved to exact native brands and verified official storefronts. This is a small diagnostic sample, not a precision or recall estimate.

The full runs exercised Qloo discovery, verified merchant Catalog research, selected exact offers, mechanism and opportunity assessment, selective Tavily use, proposal generation, save/edit and reload. The later focused replays used the exact public listing URLs observed in those runs to test the mechanism with fresh Catalog detail; they intentionally supplied no cultural relationship and therefore cannot establish a full Supported opportunity.

## Narrow correction and checks

- The merchant compatibility claim gate now permits an exact, merchant-authored statement naming the other selected product. A generic or inferred statement still leaves the claim unverified. This removes an unconditional wording veto without trusting model-proposed compatibility.
- Would-be Supported Bundles now face a second, independent counterfactual classification. It asks whether the checked selected-item facts support different concrete parts of one customer task/occasion, or merely two independent ambience/styling benefits. The response can withhold a mechanism, never supply a product fact or verdict.
- The first counterfactual wording was too strict: it rejected a synthetic towel-plus-bath-mat scenario that the scoring authority explicitly accepts. After changing the question to distinct parts of one task, the live rubric rejected a synthetic parallel rug/candle example and accepted the two-part bath task. These are rubric probes, not new benchmark ground truth.
- A fresh exact-offer replay of the Ruggable/Brooklinen pair after the corrected change produced two Needs Verification mechanism decisions for the observed candle variants. A repeat produced the same two Unknown mechanism decisions. An earlier replay after only the first challenge had one false Supported mechanism, so the repeated tests were necessary. The final samples support the correction for this pair; they do not prove deterministic behavior across all future model responses. An exact Rumpl/United by Blue Cross-Sell replay preserved two Supported product-mechanism decisions.
- Focused checks for exact merchant compatibility and product discovery, the 16-case benchmark integrity checker, syntax/whitespace checks and the signed-in local persistence/RLS/no-Catalog-payload test passed. The benchmark has no adjudicated labels, so no accuracy figure is reported.

## Limits

The full runs used an automatically selected Brand A product, not a user-chosen anchor. They do not measure end-to-end quality for every possible anchor. The full-run Supported counts are app judgments; only the named pair observations above received manual product review. Provider outputs and model classifications can vary between runs. Exact product/variant, seller and market checks remain separate from the mechanism, and a focused product replay without Qloo evidence has a Needs Verification full verdict by design. No Catalog search response, image, price, availability, or variant identifier is retained in this report.

The Our Place worker was restarted once during development and recovered through its normal lease mechanism. Its completed run used six fresh Tavily searches, including an official-site identity question and selective partner-vetting questions. It finished and cleaned up the temporary user. Its 0/12 Supported outcome does not by itself diagnose whether product retrieval or the mechanism rubric has low recall; a selected-anchor, exact-pair review would be required for that distinction.
